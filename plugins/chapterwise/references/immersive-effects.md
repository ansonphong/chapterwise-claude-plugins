# Immersive Effects Catalog

The working vocabulary of immersive experience design — named, repeatable techniques for dome shows, planetarium pieces, projection installations, and large-format work.

An **effect** is a technique that reliably produces a specific audience reaction. Simple setup, strong result, works night after night. This catalog names them so they can be chosen deliberately rather than stumbled into.

Read alongside `immersive-comfort.md`, which carries the vestibular thresholds and pacing rules that govern how these effects can be sequenced.

---

## How to read an entry

Every effect carries seven fields.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Which of the nine families it belongs to |
| **Mechanic** | What it does, concretely |
| **Targets** | The audience reaction it aims at |
| **Arc position** | Where in a show it works — `opening` · `build` · `climax` · `lull` · `close` |
| **Comfort risk** | `none` · `low` · `moderate` · `high`, with the reason |
| **Caveats** | How it fails, or what it costs |
| **Confidence** | `Documented — <source>` · `Observed practice` · `ChapterWise coinage` |

**On Confidence.** Much of immersive craft is practitioner consensus rather than measured audience research — Yu, Neafus & Wyatt say so plainly about fulldome's entire pacing doctrine. Entries are marked honestly. `Documented` means a named source describes the technique. `Observed practice` means it is demonstrably standard but not formally written up. `ChapterWise coinage` means we named it; it may still be real and useful, but no external source calls it that.

**On Comfort risk.** `high` is reserved for the three conditions the research actually supports: sustained rotation above ~20°/s, simultaneous multi-axis rotation, or translation well beyond walking pace. See `immersive-comfort.md`.

## 1. Sensory Overwhelm

Effects that flood, starve, or abruptly switch the senses.

### Drop & Burst
**Category:** Sensory Overwhelm
**Mechanic:** Sound and image fall away to near-nothing for a held beat, then a full-field, full-spectrum event lands — image, score, and any physical effects firing on the same frame.
**Targets:** Startle, then awe. The body flinches before the mind catches up.
**Arc position:** climax · build
**Comfort risk:** low — the impact is instantaneous rather than sustained
**Caveats:** Habituates fast. Twice in a show is the practical ceiling; by the third the audience is braced and nothing lands.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — the silence-then-burst pattern is widespread, this name is ours

### Full-Spectrum Fade
**Category:** Sensory Overwhelm
**Mechanic:** A scene plays desaturated or monochrome, then a story action — a door opening, a veil lifting — triggers a bloom into full saturated color.
**Targets:** Gasp. The felt sense that the world just became larger.
**Arc position:** opening · build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Spendable exactly once per show. The second use reads as a visual tic rather than a threshold.
**Confidence:** Documented — *The Wizard of Oz* (1939) is the canonical instance

### Total Blackout
**Category:** Sensory Overwhelm
**Mechanic:** Every light source is killed. No visual anchor at all, held for a sustained beat.
**Targets:** Disorientation, heightened hearing, erasure of spatial reference before a reveal.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** moderate — no rest frame whatsoever; also a genuine anxiety trigger for some guests
**Caveats:** Needs front-of-house signage in public venues. Do not hold past a few seconds without an audio anchor.
**Confidence:** Documented — standard dark-ride practice

### Sensory Layering
**Category:** Sensory Overwhelm
**Mechanic:** Deliberately stacking every available channel at once — full-field image, peak surround audio, wind, haze, seat motion — for a short "everything simultaneously" peak.
**Targets:** Overwhelm as spectacle. Visceral saturation.
**Arc position:** climax
**Comfort risk:** high — compound load across multiple systems, including motion
**Caveats:** The narrowest margin in the catalog. Miscalibrated duration tips wonder into confusion and anxiety. Keep it brief and put a lull immediately after.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — the practice is documented in sensory-design literature, the name is ours

### Surprise Sync
**Category:** Sensory Overwhelm
**Mechanic:** A physical or sonic event lands on the exact frame of a visual beat — a seat lurch as a creature strikes, a bass hit as a structure collapses.
**Targets:** Bodily confirmation of what the eye just saw. The event registers as having happened *to* the audience.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Sync drift is fatal. Audiences detect misalignment between seat and screen faster than almost any other error.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — motion-synced choreography is documented ride-film practice

## 2. Spatial Manipulation

Effects that play with scale, distance, and the audience's sense of their own size.

### Cosmic Zoom
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** A single unbroken camera move traverses many orders of magnitude — outward from a hand to a galaxy, or inward to an atom.
**Targets:** Awe at relative scale. The "small self" response.
**Arc position:** build · climax · close
**Comfort risk:** low — scale change without rotation, provided the move stays smooth
**Caveats:** Needs scale-matched assets at every step or continuity visibly breaks. Cognitively dense; pair with narration or a held beat.
**Confidence:** Documented — Eames, *Powers of Ten* (1977); a fulldome staple since

### Distance Spheres
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** Concentric reference markers — light-year rings, scale shells — are shown as the camera pulls back through them, so the scale change reads as measured rather than arbitrary.
**Targets:** Comprehension-driven awe. The audience *understands* the distance instead of merely feeling it.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Tips didactic easily. Over-explained, it converts an emotional beat into a lecture.
**Confidence:** Documented — Spitz Inc., on illustrating stellar distance and speed of light

### Encroaching Giant
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** An object begins as a distant point and grows until it fills and exceeds the field, often continuing past apparent contact with the dome surface.
**Targets:** Primal threat-awe. Audiences duck, lean back, or reach.
**Arc position:** climax · build
**Comfort risk:** low — looming without camera rotation
**Caveats:** Singular. Repeated, it degrades into generic "things fly at you."
**Confidence:** Documented — d'strict, *WAVE*, Seoul (2020)

### Surround Reveal
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** Content that began confined to a forward region expands to wrap fully around, behind, and over the audience.
**Targets:** The shift from watching to being inside. The core promise of the medium.
**Arc position:** opening · build
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Requires seamless blending; visible seams break it completely. Best held until the audience has settled.
**Confidence:** Documented — standard fulldome practice; peripheral vision engagement is the stated rationale

### Scale-Shift Juxtaposition
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** A single composition places the audience's viewpoint at a scale wildly mismatched with a nearby object — floating beside something planet-sized, standing at the foot of something enormous. Little or no camera movement.
**Targets:** Sudden awe, distinct from the gradual traversal of a Cosmic Zoom.
**Arc position:** climax
**Comfort risk:** none — the stillness is the point
**Caveats:** Needs a legible human-scale reference in frame or the scale simply doesn't read.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — the IMAX nature-documentary hero shot

### Vastness Compositing
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** A human-scale figure or object is framed against overwhelming openness — a lone silhouette before a canyon, a boat beneath a wave.
**Targets:** Textbook awe: vastness plus the need to accommodate it.
**Arc position:** opening · build · close
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** The reference figure must read instantly at a glance, or the scale cue is lost entirely.
**Confidence:** Documented — the standard awe-induction composition in immersive video research

### Forced Perspective
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** Physical or rendered elements are built at diminishing scale with distance so architecture reads as far taller or deeper than its real footprint.
**Targets:** Grandeur inside a constrained space.
**Arc position:** opening
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Works only from a controlled viewing angle. Breaks if the audience can approach or move off-axis.
**Confidence:** Documented — Disney Imagineering; the 1 : 5/8 : 1/2 storey-scale ratio

### Anamorphic Illusion
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** Imagery is pre-warped for a specific curved or angled surface so that from the intended vantage point it reads as volumetric.
**Targets:** Impossible depth on a surface the audience knows is flat.
**Arc position:** opening · climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Collapses off-axis. In a raked theatre only part of the house gets the full effect.
**Confidence:** Documented — d'strict, *WAVE*; now a genre of public digital art

### Visual Magnet
**Category:** Spatial Manipulation
**Mechanic:** A large, visually distinctive landmark is placed so it can be seen from far off, pulling attention and implied movement toward it long before it is reached.
**Targets:** Anticipation. Promises a payoff and makes the approach part of the experience.
**Arc position:** opening · build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Must be genuinely distinct. A weak one just becomes background.
**Confidence:** Documented — Disney Imagineering's "weenie"

## 3. Motion & Vection

Effects that make a seated audience feel they are moving. The most powerful family, and the only one that can make people ill. Every entry here is governed by `immersive-comfort.md`.

### Simulated Motion
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** Wide-field coherent optical flow convinces the visual system that the viewer is moving, while the inner ear correctly reports stillness.
**Targets:** Genuine felt travel without a moving vehicle. The illusion underlying every flight and dive sequence.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** **high** — this is the primary mechanism of visually-induced motion sickness; severity scales with how much of the visual field is filled
**Caveats:** The single largest comfort liability in the catalog. Provide an earth-fixed horizon. Keep rotation under ~20°/s, avoid combining rotation axes, and hold translation near walking pace.
**Confidence:** Documented — vection is extensively studied; see `immersive-comfort.md`

### The Dive
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** Sustained forward camera travel through an environment — a canyon, a nebula, a cell, a city.
**Targets:** Visceral participation. The audience is going somewhere.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** **high** — sustained vection, frequently combined with banking
**Caveats:** The fulldome workhorse and the fulldome hazard. Banking turns are the fastest route to discomfort. Slow the move, keep a horizon, and budget total dive time across the runtime.
**Confidence:** Documented — the defining fulldome camera move

### Infinite Tunnel
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** Continuous travel through a repeating or endlessly receding structure — a vortex, a corridor, a wormhole — with strong radial optical flow.
**Targets:** Hypnotic acceleration. Transition into another state or place.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** **high** — radial flow fills the periphery and there is typically no stable reference anywhere in frame
**Caveats:** Among the most nauseogenic constructions possible on a dome. Keep it short, give it a still centre to fixate on, and never stack it against another motion effect.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — the construction is common, this name is ours

### Starfield Drift
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** A field of point lights in slow ambient drift accelerates, points elongating into streaks.
**Targets:** Velocity spike. Signals departure or transition.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** moderate — strong flow, but sparse field and usually brief
**Caveats:** Heavily genre-coded by decades of science fiction. Needs re-skinning to avoid reading as pastiche.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — standard fulldome transition

### Drop-In
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** The viewpoint arrives suddenly inside a scene already in motion, with no establishing approach.
**Targets:** Immediate immersion. No preamble, no orientation period.
**Arc position:** opening · build
**Comfort risk:** moderate — the discontinuity is abrupt, though brief
**Caveats:** Costs the audience their bearings. Follow quickly with a stable frame or a horizon.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage

### Liquid Flow
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** Simulated fluid — water, smoke, ink, particles — carries one image into the next, dissolving and reforming.
**Targets:** Organic transition that avoids a hard cut.
**Arc position:** build · lull
**Comfort risk:** low — motion is local rather than a whole-field flow
**Caveats:** Computationally heavy. Overused, every transition starts to feel the same.
**Confidence:** Documented — teamLab, *Universe of Water Particles*

### Parallax Layering
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** Foreground, midground and background move at different apparent rates, near objects passing quickly while far ones drift.
**Targets:** Depth that reads as real space rather than a painted backdrop.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** In stereo, needs careful convergence or it produces eye strain.
**Confidence:** Documented — standard ride-film practice

### Vestibular Drop
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** A steep visual descent paired with a physical seat drop or pressure change.
**Targets:** Genuine falling sensation. Adrenaline.
**Arc position:** climax
**Comfort risk:** **high** — deliberately approaches the discomfort threshold
**Caveats:** Documented practitioner framing is that this is held *just short* of nausea. Not for general-audience family programming without a shorter hold.
**Confidence:** Documented — simulator ride-film practice

### Smoothed Path
**Category:** Motion & Vection
**Mechanic:** High-frequency shake is removed from the visual camera path and delegated entirely to physical seat motion, so what the eye sees stays smooth while the body feels the jolts.
**Targets:** Thrill without compounding the sickness cues.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** none — this is itself a mitigation
**Caveats:** Only available where a motion base exists.
**Confidence:** Documented — established ride-film technique

## 4. Dome-Native Craft

Techniques that exist because the surface is a dome and the audience can look anywhere. Largely absent from general effect vocabularies.

### Darkness Adaptation Fade
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** House lights and sound descend to black over a period of minutes before content begins.
**Targets:** Anticipation, collective hush — and genuine physiological dark adaptation, which the eye actually requires.
**Arc position:** opening
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Needs a clear button at the end — a first star, a first tone — or it reads as a technical fault.
**Confidence:** Documented — the classical planetarium opening

### Star-by-Star Reveal
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** Stars appear individually or in slow clusters rather than snapping to a full sky.
**Targets:** Wonder. A sky that is arriving rather than being displayed.
**Arc position:** opening · lull
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Over-familiar to regular planetarium audiences; needs a fresh framing reason to look up.
**Confidence:** Documented — the classical planetarium curtain-raiser

### Zenith Reveal
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** The significant event is staged directly overhead, requiring the audience to physically tilt their heads back.
**Targets:** Embodied awe. The body's own movement becomes part of the moment. Flat cinema cannot ask for this.
**Arc position:** climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Entirely dependent on seating rake. Verify sightlines from the back row before committing a beat to the zenith.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — derived from dome-master geometry

### Horizon Anchor
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** A stable horizon line is established and held, giving the visual and vestibular systems a shared reference for which way is down.
**Targets:** Comfort. Invisible when working.
**Arc position:** opening · build · lull
**Comfort risk:** none — this is the single most validated mitigation available
**Caveats:** Its *absence* is the problem. Any sustained motion sequence without a horizon is a comfort risk by construction.
**Confidence:** Documented — earth-fixed horizons significantly reduce visually-induced motion sickness where a bare fixation point does not

### Horizon Break
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** The established horizon is deliberately tilted, removed, or multiplied — two horizons on an alien world, a horizon that rolls away.
**Targets:** Controlled destabilization. Signals otherworldliness or loss of control.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** **high** — deliberately removes the primary comfort reference
**Caveats:** Earn it first. Breaking a horizon that was never established is just disorientation. Restore it promptly.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — the inverse of documented rest-frame practice

### Continuous Journey
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** An unbroken camera path replaces a sequence of cuts, carrying the audience from one scene into the next without an edit.
**Targets:** Uninterrupted presence. Nothing ever vanishes from under the eye.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** moderate — sustained movement, though smooth and predictable
**Caveats:** The defining fulldome structure — some shows are two shots for an entire runtime. Overdone it becomes soporific.
**Confidence:** Documented — the "narrative journey"; see Yu, Neafus & Wyatt

### Match Cut
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** Two shots are bridged by a shared shape, motion, or position, so the edit is nearly invisible and the eye stays where it was.
**Targets:** A cut that costs the audience nothing.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** On a dome, the match must also preserve *direction* — the point of interest should remain in the same region of the sky across the cut.
**Confidence:** Documented — recommended fulldome practice; repeating a few frames of action across the cut strengthens it

### Orbit Move
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** The camera circles a subject instead of cutting between reverse angles, keeping a continuous frame of reference through a conversation or confrontation.
**Targets:** Coverage without the disorientation of shot-reverse-shot on a wraparound surface.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** moderate — sustained yaw; hold it under ~20°/s
**Caveats:** Single-axis only. Combining orbit with any other rotation is exactly the multi-axis case the research flags hardest.
**Confidence:** Documented — recommended fulldome alternative to reverse-angle cutting

### Reveal by Occlusion
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** A foreground element hides something behind it; camera or object motion slowly exposes it. Surprise without an edit.
**Targets:** Discovery, at the audience's own pace.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Needs enough dwell time for the reveal to be noticed across a wide field.
**Confidence:** Documented — fulldome practice

### Lap Dissolve
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** Two images cross-fade across the dome rather than cutting.
**Targets:** Transition without instant subtraction — nothing the audience was looking at abruptly disappears.
**Arc position:** build · lull
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Slower than a cut by design. Budget the time.
**Confidence:** Documented — inherited from stacked-projector planetarium practice

### Safe-Area Staging
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** Story-critical content is placed within the region of the dome visible from every seat without head-turning.
**Targets:** Nobody misses the plot because of where they sat.
**Arc position:** all
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** The safe area differs per venue and per seating geometry. Concentric seating has no shared forward direction at all.
**Confidence:** Documented — built from measured audience-visibility maps in real theatres

### Held Frame
**Category:** Dome-Native Craft
**Mechanic:** The camera slows or stops and the shot is simply held, inviting the audience to look around the environment on their own.
**Targets:** Rest. Undirected exploration. The audience's attention is permitted to wander.
**Arc position:** lull
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** No single image should hold beyond roughly thirty seconds without some variation.
**Confidence:** Documented — fulldome rest practice; attention naturally disperses in extended static shots

## 5. Meta & Frame-Breaking

Effects that acknowledge the medium, the room, or the audience.

### Breaking the Dome
**Category:** Meta & Frame-Breaking
**Mechanic:** The show acknowledges its own surface — the dome is named, revealed, dissolved, or treated as a physical object within the fiction.
**Targets:** Novelty jolt, or the opposite: proving the frame is gone deepens immersion.
**Arc position:** opening · climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Needs consistent internal logic. Is this world always aware of itself, or is this a single rupture? Ambiguity reads as sloppiness.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — dome-specific variant of fourth-wall practice

### Portal
**Category:** Meta & Frame-Breaking
**Mechanic:** A doorway, gate, or aperture is staged as the literal boundary between before and after.
**Targets:** Marks the point of no return. Primes the audience that everything past this changes.
**Arc position:** opening · build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Must be singular. Repeated portals dilute the device into set dressing.
**Confidence:** Documented — threshold staging in dark rides and immersive exhibits

### Mirror Effect
**Category:** Meta & Frame-Breaking
**Mechanic:** Reflective surfaces or rendered reflections place the audience's own image inside the work.
**Targets:** Personal implication. The audience is in the scene.
**Arc position:** climax · close
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** In a dome, hard to achieve without physical staging. More available to installations than projected shows.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — infinity-mirror installations are a documented genre, this framing is ours

### Direct Address
**Category:** Meta & Frame-Breaking
**Mechanic:** A narrator or character speaks to the audience as an audience.
**Targets:** Intimacy, complicity, surprise.
**Arc position:** opening · close
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Planetarium narration does this by default, which blunts it. To land as an *effect* it has to break an established distance.
**Confidence:** Documented — standard across immersive theatre and planetarium practice

### Audience as Participant
**Category:** Meta & Frame-Breaking
**Mechanic:** The design treats the audience's presence, position, or movement as part of the piece rather than incidental to it.
**Targets:** Raised personal stakes. This is happening to me, not near me.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Comfort with participation varies enormously. Signal opt-in and opt-out clearly.
**Confidence:** Documented — immersive theatre practice

## 6. Temporal

Effects that manipulate the audience's sense of elapsed time.

### Time-Lapse
**Category:** Temporal
**Mechanic:** Slow processes are compressed — clouds racing, structures rising, seasons turning.
**Targets:** Vast time made legible.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Low risk, low novelty. Used across a whole show it flattens pacing variety.
**Confidence:** Documented — standard cinematographic technique

### Day-to-Night Passage
**Category:** Temporal
**Mechanic:** A single continuous move spans daylight through dusk into full night, with exposure ramped so there is no visible brightness step.
**Targets:** Seamless passage of time without a cut. On a dome it also motivates the transition from daytime content into the night sky.
**Arc position:** opening · build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Technically the hardest time-lapse variant. A poor exposure ramp is immediately obvious.
**Confidence:** Documented — the "holy grail" time-lapse

### Collapse & Rebuild
**Category:** Temporal
**Mechanic:** Something is shown deteriorating or destroyed, then reversed or cut to a restored state.
**Targets:** Before-and-after contrast. Loss followed by hope, or the reverse as warning.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Emotionally blunt if the rebuild is unearned.
**Confidence:** ChapterWise coinage — the component techniques are standard, this pairing is ours

### Era Match-Cut
**Category:** Temporal
**Mechanic:** Framing holds fixed while everything within it changes across cuts — architecture, dress, technology — implying centuries passing from a stationary vantage.
**Targets:** Continuity through change. A stable anchor makes vast spans comprehensible.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** The anchor must be genuinely unchanged, or the effect reads as a series of unrelated shots.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — standard film grammar applied to dome content

### Deep-Time Zoom
**Category:** Temporal
**Mechanic:** A Cosmic Zoom with a time axis — as the view pulls back through space, the clock also runs, showing formation, evolution, decay.
**Targets:** Compound awe. Scale and time at once.
**Arc position:** climax · close
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Cognitively very dense. Needs narration or a held beat afterward, or the audience loses the thread entirely.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — a fulldome origins-show staple

## 7. Psychological & Pattern

Effects that work on recognition, symmetry, and interiority.

### Hidden Geometry
**Category:** Psychological & Pattern
**Mechanic:** An apparently chaotic field — foliage, crowd, brushwork, stars — resolves into a recognizable pattern, face, or structure as the view pulls back or the pattern completes.
**Targets:** The recognition reward. Delight at order emerging from noise.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** The payoff must be unambiguous. A vague reveal reads as a rendering artifact.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — widespread in immersive art installations

### Inside the Body
**Category:** Psychological & Pattern
**Mechanic:** The viewpoint shrinks to microscopic scale and travels through an interior landscape — bloodstream, cell, neural tissue, crystal lattice.
**Targets:** Uncanny wonder. The ordinary contains an entire world.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** moderate — usually built as a flythrough, inheriting that risk
**Caveats:** Abstract without a reason to be there. Give it a mission or a subject.
**Confidence:** Documented — *Fantastic Voyage* (1966) established the form

### Mandala Morph
**Category:** Psychological & Pattern
**Mechanic:** Radially symmetrical patterns evolve continuously, often locked to the score.
**Targets:** Hypnotic, meditative, trance-adjacent states.
**Arc position:** lull
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Generic without a conceptual tie to the surrounding content. Easily reads as a screensaver.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — common in ambient and interlude sequences

## 8. Physical & Environmental

Effects delivered through the body and the room rather than the screen.

### Environmental Sync
**Category:** Physical & Environmental
**Mechanic:** Wind, mist, scent, or radiant heat fire in time with on-screen events.
**Targets:** Cross-modal confirmation. Scent in particular triggers association below conscious awareness.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Scent is logistically hard — ventilation clearing time limits how often it can change before smells smear together.
**Confidence:** Documented — standard themed-entertainment practice

### Haptic Seat
**Category:** Physical & Environmental
**Mechanic:** Seats vibrate, tilt, or deliver air and water bursts synchronized to specific beats.
**Targets:** Physical confirmation of impact.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** moderate — adds real vestibular input to visual motion
**Caveats:** Sync tolerance is very tight. Choreograph three to five key moments rather than tracking everything, or the effect fatigues.
**Confidence:** Documented — 4D cinema practice

### Infrasound Rumble
**Category:** Physical & Environmental
**Mechanic:** Very low frequency energy, felt more than heard, tied to visual impact events.
**Targets:** Chest-level weight. Makes large things feel genuinely large.
**Arc position:** climax
**Comfort risk:** low
**Caveats:** Constrained by the building. Rumble travels to neighbouring spaces.
**Confidence:** Observed practice — widely used, rarely formally documented

### Mist Screen
**Category:** Physical & Environmental
**Mechanic:** A curtain of atomized water serves as a mid-air projection surface, letting an image float in open space with no visible support.
**Targets:** An image that appears to exist without a screen.
**Arc position:** climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Airflow-sensitive. Ventilation drafts distort it.
**Confidence:** Documented — installation and dark-ride practice

### Pepper's Ghost
**Category:** Physical & Environmental
**Mechanic:** Angled glass reflects a hidden, brightly lit subject so it appears as a translucent figure within a physical set.
**Targets:** Apparition. Seamless blend of real space and impossible occupant.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Centuries old and widely recognized. Effectiveness now rests entirely on staging and story context.
**Confidence:** Documented — the canonical dark-ride illusion

### Reactive Surface
**Category:** Physical & Environmental
**Mechanic:** Ultraviolet or otherwise selective illumination activates materials invisible under normal light, so scenery appears from nothing.
**Targets:** Emergence. Otherworldly colour separation.
**Arc position:** build
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Dated on its own. Modern use pairs it with projection.
**Confidence:** Documented — classic dark-ride practice

## 9. Audio Spatialization

The dome's other surface. Sound can direct the eye before the eye finds the subject.

### Spatial Audio Object
**Category:** Audio Spatialization
**Mechanic:** Individual sounds carry 3D position data and are rendered to the venue's speaker array, including overhead.
**Targets:** Precise moving point sources — something passing overhead, a voice circling the room.
**Arc position:** all
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Needs a real array to realize fully. Degrades gracefully but loses precision on minimal systems.
**Confidence:** Documented — object-based audio is standard in premium immersive venues

### Directional Cue
**Category:** Audio Spatialization
**Mechanic:** A sound is placed to precede or match a visual event's position, telling the audience where to look before the eye arrives.
**Targets:** Pre-attentive orientation. Solves the "they can look anywhere" problem without moving the image.
**Arc position:** all
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** No shared audio standard exists across dome venues, so this travels less reliably than visual cueing. Do not make a plot point depend on it alone.
**Confidence:** Documented — with the portability caveat noted in fulldome literature

### Motion-Reinforcing Audio
**Category:** Audio Spatialization
**Mechanic:** Environmental sound moves in sync with visual optical flow during a travel sequence.
**Targets:** Strengthens the self-motion illusion beyond what image alone achieves.
**Arc position:** build · climax
**Comfort risk:** moderate — a stronger illusion means stronger susceptibility
**Caveats:** Amplifies both the effect and its cost. Count it against the motion budget.
**Confidence:** Documented — moving sound matched to visual landmarks measurably enhances vection

### Hard Out
**Category:** Audio Spatialization
**Mechanic:** A cue crescendos and cuts directly to silence on a picture beat.
**Targets:** Maximum impact at a sync point. The silence lands harder than the crescendo did.
**Arc position:** climax
**Comfort risk:** none
**Caveats:** Needs an exact sync point. Approximate timing wastes it.
**Confidence:** Documented — named film-scoring technique

### The Held Silence
**Category:** Audio Spatialization
**Mechanic:** Narration stops, score comes up or falls away, and the dome holds a simple image while nothing is explained.
**Targets:** Recovery. The audience metabolizes what just happened.
**Arc position:** lull
**Comfort risk:** none — this is the recovery mechanism
**Caveats:** The most under-used effect in the catalog, and the one the physiology most demands. Five to ten seconds at every cornerstone beat. Planetarium craft treats a moment of simply appreciating the sky as a mandatory final check on any script.
**Confidence:** Documented — explicit planetarium scriptwriting guidance

A show is not a list of effects, it is a shape. This is the quick index for finding the right kind of beat.

## Adding to this catalog

New effects are welcome and expected — the medium is not finished. When adding one:

1. Give it a name a director could say out loud in a production meeting.
2. Fill all seven fields. An entry without `Comfort risk` and `Arc position` cannot be reasoned over.
3. Mark `Confidence` honestly. `ChapterWise coinage` is not a lesser status — several of the strongest entries here are ours. It is simply true.
4. If it involves camera motion, check it against `immersive-comfort.md` before assigning a risk level.
